The city of Mathurā was peaceful, and gentle hope shimmered in the cool dawn air. In a royal home scented with flowers and filled with soft lamplight, Devakī gave birth to her very first child, a beautiful baby boy. They named him Kīrtimān. He looked as soft as a lotus petal, and his gentle cries brought smiles—and worry—to his parents’ faces alike. Devakī cradled her newborn, her eyes shining with deep love but also a shadow of sadness. She remembered the promise she and her husband Vasudeva had made to her brother, Kaṁsa. Fearing for himself, Kaṁsa had demanded that every child born to Devakī be brought to him at once, to be handed over without question. Even though no one wanted danger to come to their children or home, Vasudeva bravely accepted this task. He understood that a good and safe promise deserves honest care, and it is during the hardest times that dharma—one’s right action—matters most. For a little while, the home was filled only with the soothing sounds of Kīrtimān’s breathing and the quiet shuffling of the nursemaids. Vasudeva stood tall and gentle, looking at his son. He spoke softly to Devakī: “My dear, our path is difficult, but this is our dharma. We must keep our word, even amidst sorrow.” Devakī held little Kīrtimān close, brushing a blessing across his forehead and whispering a prayer to Lord Krishna for protection. Her heart trembled even as she wished her child safety. With calm, Vasudeva bathed Kīrtimān in warm fragrant water, wrapped him in the softest blue cloth, and placed a tiny protective charm near his heart. The golden lamps in their home flickered, their light like hopeful stars above them. Devakī kissed her son and handed him to Vasudeva, her eyes full of both hope and worry. Before he left, Vasudeva knelt beside his wife, promising to return, though neither spoke what felt heaviest in their hearts. Together, silently, they prayed for their family’s safety. As the sun slowly rose over Mathurā, Vasudeva stepped into the awakening streets, his sandals making soft echoes on the stone paths. The air was cool and quiet, with doves cooing under distant temple roofs. Shopkeepers put out spices, milkmen carried their pots, and gentle animals wandered peacefully. Each sight felt precious, as if Vasudeva was seeing the city through new eyes. With every step, he whispered to Kīrtimān, “You are braver than you know, my dear child.” Reaching Kaṁsa’s grand palace, Vasudeva paused before tall gates rising high and golden pillars sparkling in early sunlight. Led through echoing halls, he soon stood before Kaṁsa—Devakī’s troubled brother, his eyes dark with fear and pride. Kaṁsa looked up in startled surprise. “So soon, Vasudeva? Is this truly your first child?” Vasudeva nodded, speaking with quiet strength. “Yes, noble Kaṁsa. This is Kīrtimān, our firstborn. I have come as I promised.” Kaṁsa stared at the bundle in Vasudeva’s arms. For a moment, holding his own nephew, even his hardened heart faltered. Kīrtimān gurgled softly, unconcerned by the adults' tension. Kaṁsa’s hands, used to commands and worry, surprisingly softened as he saw Kīrtimān’s tiny fingers curl. Kaṁsa asked, “This is Kīrtimān, not the eighth?” Vasudeva replied, “Yes, the prophecy spoke of our eighth child, not the first. Please, see for yourself—he means you no harm.” For a moment, the room was filled only with Kīrtimān’s gentle breaths, as even fate seemed hushed. Kaṁsa, his face troubled, gazed silently into Kīrtimān’s small face. Before him was the innocence of a new soul—his own sister’s son. After a long stillness, Kaṁsa returned Kīrtimān to Vasudeva. “Take him home,” he said with a wary sigh. “It is only the eighth child whom I must watch for.” Relief and gratitude flashed through Vasudeva’s heart, but he remained alert. He bowed with respect, thanking Kaṁsa, and held Kīrtimān tightly—knowing that mercy could quickly disappear in a heart ruled by fear and pride. Quietly, he left the palace, his caution growing. The city outside seemed both beautiful and uncertain. Back home, Devakī rushed to embrace her child. Her face shone with both joy and anxious tears as she pressed Kīrtimān to her heart. “You are safe, my love,” she whispered, but Vasudeva’s gentle hand touched her shoulder to remind her—not all was well forever. The family remained careful, not trusting that Kaṁsa’s worries had ended. In the evening, as soft lamps glimmered and the city’s sounds faded, the household gathered near, quietly giving gratitude for this day together. Vasudeva watched over his family, resolved to stay truthful, alert, and wise. He taught by his shining example that courage is not the absence of fear, but devotion to what is right—especially when it is hardest. The family’s safety was not guaranteed, but their hearts trusted in Krishna, and in Vasudeva’s steady, honest love. Vasudeva brought Kīrtimān, Devakī and Vasudeva’s first son, to Kaṁsa because he was committed to truthfulness and duty.
