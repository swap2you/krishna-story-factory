Tonight, dear children, let us journey to the wondrous city of Mathurā, where gentle morning sunlight was beginning to shine. In a beautiful home decorated with blooming flowers, Devakī held her firstborn son, Kīrtimān. His soft cheeks glowed, and his tiny hands clutched Devakī’s finger. <break time="1.0s" /> Devakī smiled and wept at once, for she remembered the difficult promise made years ago: she and her husband Vasudeva had sworn to deliver every child born to Kaṁsa, her brother. An old prophecy had scared Kaṁsa—he heard Devakī’s eighth son would change his fate forever. But now, in this quiet moment, only baby Kīrtimān’s gurgles filled the home. Vasudeva, calm and wise, knelt beside Devakī. “Beloved, our promise is hard, but we should honor a good and safe promise. Truthfulness is our path—even in sorrow.” <break time="1.0s" /> Vasudeva bathed Kīrtimān gently, wrapped him in a blue cloth, and placed a small protective charm near his heart. Before leaving, Devakī pressed her lips to Kīrtimān’s forehead and whispered a soft prayer to Krishna. <break time="1.0s" /> Quietly, Vasudeva stepped onto dawn-lit streets, carrying Kīrtimān close to his chest. Shopkeepers swept their doors, birds called from temple rooftops, and the world awoke slowly. Vasudeva’s heart was heavy, but he whispered, "You are so brave, my small son." <break time="1.0s" /> At the tall gates of Kaṁsa’s glittering palace, Vasudeva was welcomed with wary glances. He strode through grand halls to where Kaṁsa waited, his face tight with worry. “Is this really your first child?” Kaṁsa demanded. With steady kindness, Vasudeva nodded. “Yes, noble Kaṁsa. This is Kīrtimān. The prophecy spoke only of the eighth child.” <break time="1.0s" /> Kaṁsa peered at the newborn. For a moment, Kaṁsa’s heart softened as he gazed at Kīrtimān’s tiny face—his own nephew. The palace was quiet as Kaṁsa decided—finally, he gave Kīrtimān back. “Take your child home. This is not the one I fear.” <break time="1.0s" /> Vasudeva bowed deeply and returned home as quietly as he’d come. Though relief filled his heart, he did not relax. He knew Kaṁsa’s fears could rise again. <break time="1.0s" /> Back at home, Devakī clung to her baby, joy and worry swirling inside her. Vasudeva’s hand on her shoulder reminded her—they must stay wise and careful. Their safety was only for this day. <break time="1.0s" /> Vasudeva and Devakī thanked Krishna and held Kīrtimān close, trusting their faith and Vasudeva’s honest courage to keep guiding them, even when the future was uncertain. <break time="1.0s" /> Vasudeva brought Kīrtimān, Devakī and Vasudeva’s first son, to Kaṁsa because he was committed to truthfulness and duty. Astonished by Vasudeva's honesty, Kaṁsa returned Kīrtimān, at least for now, because the warning concerned the eighth child. <break time="1.0s" /> Vasudeva’s brave heart showed that truthfulness is something we practice even when keeping a promise is hard. The family was relieved in this moment, but Vasudeva watched and waited, never assuming they were safe forever. <break time="1.0s" /> Vasudeva's choice was difficult, yet he walked forward calmly and kept his word. His example shows that truthfulness is not merely something we say; it is something we practice when a promise is hard to keep.
